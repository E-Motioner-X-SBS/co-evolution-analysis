\section{Detailed Results: SARS-CoV-2 Omicron Spike Analysis}
\label{sec:results-spike}

Before presenting cross-protein transfer results on the PSICOV150 benchmark,
we present the complete analysis of our primary target: the SARS-CoV-2
Omicron variant Spike protein.  This serves both as a biological case study
and as a demonstration of the full pipeline applied to a single, deeply
characterised protein family.

The extreme conservation of the Spike protein (98.4\% of positions invariant)
reflects the structural constraints imposed by its trimeric assembly and
receptor-binding function.  Only 21 positions carry appreciable evolutionary
variability, concentrated in the N-terminal domain (NTD) and receptor-binding
domain (RBD) that are targets of antibody-mediated immune pressure.

A critical observation is that the top five pairs by mutation-only MI (the
442--498 cluster) have very low full MI (0.000--0.039), indicating that their
covariation is driven by mutations away from the reference rather than by the
reference itself.  This is the signature of lineage-specific covariation:
different viral variants carry different combinations at these positions,
producing strong mutation correlation without necessarily implying physical
contact.

\subsection{Prime Implicants and Essential Rules}
Quine--McCluskey minimisation over the ten co-evolving pairs (padded
$32\times32$ mutation maps, reference pair and padding as don't-cares)
produces 36 prime implicants, of which 35 are essential; the essential set is
also the minimum cover.  Nearly every observed co-mutation is an isolated
cell of its map, so it has exactly one covering implicant; only a few cubes
merge two residues on one axis, e.g.
\begin{equation}
	\text{pos}495 \in \{\text{Q},\text{R}\} \;\wedge\; \text{pos}498 = \text{G}.
\end{equation}
The minimisation therefore compresses the observed combinations very little:
the circuit is close to a list of the double mutants seen in the alignment.
(A first version reported ``2 essential'' implicants; that count came from a
bookkeeping defect in the minimiser, corrected in \texttt{kmap\_sbm} and
checked against brute force.)  Positions are 0-based alignment columns; in
the RBD, Wuhan-Hu-1 residue $=$ column $- 2$.

\subsection{Flipped Circuit: Forbidden Combinations}
Applying Quine--McCluskey to the flipped maps (ON = never observed, OFF =
observed, DC = reference) produces 246 never-observed cubes over the ten
pairs (52 essential).  With 1{,}299 sequences of which only 299 are unique,
all from one clonal population, a combination that was never observed is
absence of evidence, not a demonstrated negative-selection constraint.  In
the separation-matched 3D test below, the NTD pairs (210, 215) and (212, 215)
are not in contact more often than other residue pairs at the same sequence
separation, so the earlier steric reading of their forbidden cells is not
supported.

\subsection{Structural Validation Against 299 Homology Models}
We mapped each alignment column to the corresponding residue in the best-GMQE
SWISS-MODEL model of each of the 299 unique sequences (column-to-residue map
verified against the model's target sequence) and called a contact when the
C$\beta$--C$\beta$ distance is below 8\,\AA{}.  Two features of these data
decide how the contact rates can be tested.  First, most candidate pairs are
only 3--5 residues apart, where a contact is close to automatic; the expected
rate must come from residue pairs at the \emph{same} separation (all of
chain~A, and within $\pm30$ residues of the pair), not from a control pooled
over all separations.  Second, the models are built on 11 templates, one of
which (8d55) supplies 203 of the 299 models, so the independent units are the
templates, not the models.  Table~\ref{tab:spike-3d} uses both
(\texttt{analysis/spike\_3d\_matched\_check.py}).

None of the ten co-evolving pairs is in contact more often than residue pairs
at the same separation: the five RBD pairs of the 442--498 cluster are never
in contact, and the NTD pairs sit at the rate their separation predicts (the
NTD separations vary because of the Omicron deletions).  The three pairs that
are enriched, (407, 410), (500, 503) and (503, 507), are $i,i{+}3$ and
$i,i{+}4$ pairs that are close in every Spike structure; their contact is a
property of the fold and does not single out co-evolution.  An earlier
version of this table reported 2.3--11.3$\times$ enrichments with
$p < 10^{-6}$, from a pooled-separation control and a binomial test over the
299 models; with matched controls and templates as units that evidence does
not hold.  The strongest full-MI pair (373, 378; MI $= 0.807$) is not in
contact either.

\subsection{Perplexity Ratio as a Structural Predictor}
Among all sequence-derived metrics evaluated on the Spike, the perplexity
ratio achieves the highest Spearman correlation with native-contact fraction:
\begin{equation}
	\rho(\text{ratio}, \text{contact}) = 0.670, \quad p = 0.006.
\end{equation}
Near-deterministic pairs (ratio $> 1.5$) are enriched for contacts at
$1.80\times$, while low-ratio pairs show no enrichment.  This suggests that
perplexity ratio captures a property of coevolution---near-determinism of the
amino-acid relationship---that raw MI does not distinguish from noisy
covariation.  The correlation is over 15 pairs and uses the per-model contact
fractions of the pooled-control analysis, so it carries the same template
caveat as the original 3D table and should be read as exploratory.

\subsection{Partner Prediction and Lineage Linkage}
For every sequence carrying a mutation at one of the ten pairs, the
constraint function $C(a,b) = \ln\frac{P(a,b)}{P(a)P(b)}$, built from the
other 1{,}298 sequences, predicts its residue at $j$ from its residue at $i$
(argmax over partners that co-occur with $a$).  Leave-one-out accuracy is
99.4\% (3{,}241/3{,}259; conditional-frequency baseline $\arg\max_b P(b\mid a)$:
65.5\%).  The earlier 9.24\% came from taking the argmax of an
$\varepsilon$-smoothed $C$ over all 20 residues, where a never-observed partner
scores $\ln(1/P(a)) > 0$ and wins.

The high accuracy is linkage within one clonal population, not a portable
rule.  Withholding, together with the test sequence, every training sequence
within $h$ mismatches of it (\texttt{analysis/spike\_lineage\_loo.py};
median pairwise distance 1, 90th percentile 12) gives:

\subsection{Conserved Core Analysis}
Of the 1{,}276 alignment columns, 1{,}255 are conserved ($H < 0.3$).  These
columns encode the structural core of the Spike protein, including the fusion
peptide, heptad repeats, and transmembrane domain.  While they generate no
coevolutionary signal (their entropy is too low for meaningful MI), they
carry structural information through their absolute constraint: any mutation
at these positions is likely deleterious and rapidly purged.  Our complete
circuit incorporates this information through the conservation-type feature
dimension, which enriches cons/cons column pairs for native contacts by
$3.2\times$.

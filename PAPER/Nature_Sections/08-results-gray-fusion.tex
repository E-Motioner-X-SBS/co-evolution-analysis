\section{Gray Adjacency Enrichment and Rank Fusion with DCA}
\label{sec:res-gray-fusion}

\subsection{Gray adjacency among native contacts}

Among 47{,}430 native-contact pairs across 150 PSICOV proteins, 22.83\% are
Gray-adjacent ($d_G = 1$), compared to a composition-controlled background of
19.37\%, yielding an enrichment of $1.178\times$ ($p = 1.1 \times 10^{-77}$,
two-sided exact binomial).  Bootstrap confidence intervals from protein-level
resampling span [22.27\%, 23.40\%].

A permutation test over random relabelings of the twenty amino-acid codes
demonstrated that this enrichment is specific to the He/Gray assignment:
random code permutations yield a mean enrichment of $0.961 \pm 0.095$, placing
the true encoding at $z = 2.51$ above the null distribution.  An earlier
analysis restricted to a single protein found the same point estimate but
failed to reach significance due to insufficient sample size ($n = 38$).

\subsection{Rank fusion with DCA}

Re-run unchanged, the published experiment reproduces: rank-sum fusion
reaches 0.204 against 0.169 for its DCA input (paired difference $+0.036$,
95\% CI $[0.017, 0.054]$).  That DCA input is defective, however: it weights
pair frequencies by $W^3$ and takes the Frobenius norm outside the zero-sum
gauge, and at 0.169 it is no better than APC-corrected MI (0.189, $p = 0.24$).
A standard mean-field DCA on the same alignments reaches 0.566 (direct
information) and 0.692 (zero-sum-gauge APC-Frobenius).  Fused with either, the
K-map prior lowers precision@L/5 (to 0.471 and 0.565; $-0.095$ and $-0.127$,
$p < 10^{-12}$), while raising AUC slightly.  Equal-weight rank aggregation
with any much weaker score dilutes a strong one (DI with MIp: 0.520).  The
published gain reflects a weak DCA being partly compensated; it is not
evidence that the circuit carries information DCA lacks.  The earlier
``mechanistic decomposition'' counts have no surviving analysis and are
withdrawn.

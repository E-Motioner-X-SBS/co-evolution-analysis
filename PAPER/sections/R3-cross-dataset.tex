\section{Cross-Dataset Validation}
\label{sec:cross-dataset}

To assess generalisation beyond the PSICOV150 benchmark, we evaluated contact
prediction on five additional datasets spanning diverse protein families,
alignment depths, and structural sources.

The PSICOV150 rows were recomputed with the correct column map (PSICOV
alignments are numbered like the native chain; the first alignment row is a
gapped homologue, so mapping through it was partly wrong) and with a random
control that always draws inside the chain (\texttt{analysis/psicov\_top\_pairs\_check.py}):
mean precision 0.071, mean enrichment 2.38$\times$, 67 of 150 targets above
2$\times$ (first reported 0.066 and 2.49$\times$).  The GPCRdb and unit-test rows
use the same first-row mapping and control and were not re-run (their
alignments are not part of this repository); they rest on 20 pairs each and
should be read as anecdotes.

The $\beta_2$-adrenergic receptor (b2AR) shows the strongest enrichment
(18.5$\times$), consistent with its deep alignment (136 sequences) and
well-resolved crystal structure.  The inconclusive results for DHFR and
5-HT1A reflect known limitations: DHFR top pairs fall in insert-only columns
absent from PDB 4P3R, while 7E2X covers only a 219-residue TM-core fragment
of 5-HT1A.

\subsection{Omicron Spike Structural Validation}

For the Omicron Spike, the co-evolving pairs were checked against the 299
SWISS-MODEL models with same-separation controls and templates as the unit
(Section~\ref{sec:results-spike}, Table~\ref{tab:spike-3d}).  None of the ten
co-evolving pairs is in contact more often than residue pairs at the same
sequence separation.  The pairs (407, 410), (500, 503) and (503, 507) are
enriched against that control, but they are $i,i{+}3$/$i,i{+}4$ pairs that are
close in every Spike structure.  The earlier table here (seven pairs at
3.7--11.3$\times$, including a ``DCA-only'' pair (454, 495)) used a control
pooled over separations and a binomial test over models; mfDCA on this
alignment has $M_{\text{eff}} = 1$ (every sequence lies within 80\% identity
of every other), so it cannot single out pairs either.

\subsection{Literature Calibration}

Our ground-truth pipeline was calibrated against published benchmarks:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
	\item PSICOV published prec@L/5 = 0.73 (Jones Table~1); our measurement:
	      \textbf{0.727} ($\Delta = 0.003$).
	\item PSICOV long-range prec@L/5 $\approx$ 0.68; ours: \textbf{0.640}.
	\item Abstract claim ``118/150 targets $\geq 0.5$'' confirmed by our count.
\end{itemize}

This calibration confirms that our evaluation pipeline reproduces published
benchmarks, eliminating concerns about implementation-specific biases in the
ground truth.

\subsection{mfDCA Benchmark Validation}

Our first mean-field DCA implementation achieved prec@L/5 = 0.169 on
PSICOV150, which we described as consistent with mean-field benchmarks.  It
was not: a corrected mean-field DCA reaches 0.566 (direct information) and
0.692 (zero-sum-gauge APC-Frobenius) on the same alignments, against PSICOV's
0.727.  The remaining defects ($W^3$ pair weighting, non-zero-sum gauge) and
their effect on the rank-fusion result are described in
Section~\ref{sec:results-fusion}.
